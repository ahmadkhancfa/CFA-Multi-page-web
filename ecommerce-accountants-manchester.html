VAT for Ecommerce Businesses

VAT is one of the most complex areas for ecommerce businesses, particularly those selling across borders. The rules depend on where your customers are, what you are selling, and where your stock is held. The current UK VAT registration threshold is £90,000 of taxable turnover in a rolling 12-month period. HMRC's VAT threshold guidance explains when you must register.

✓UK VAT registration and return preparation
✓Making Tax Digital (MTD) compliant VAT submissions
✓VAT threshold monitoring and registration timing advice
✓Partial exemption calculations where applicable
✓Reverse charge VAT for B2B digital services
✓Distance selling rules for UK to UK Crown Dependencies

Selling to EU Customers — VAT OSS

Since July 2021, UK businesses selling goods to consumers in EU member states may need to register for VAT in those countries or use the EU's One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme. The rules depend on the value and nature of the sales.

✓Assessment of EU VAT obligations
✓OSS registration support in relevant EU member states
✓Quarterly OSS return preparation
✓Distance selling threshold monitoring per EU country
✓IOSS for consignment imports under €150 into the EU
✓Advice on using fulfilment centres in EU member states

UK businesses selling through Amazon FBA who store stock in EU fulfilment centres may have VAT registration obligations in those countries regardless of sales volumes. This is a common area where ecommerce businesses are unknowingly non-compliant.

Import VAT & Customs Duties

Ecommerce businesses importing stock into the UK face import VAT and potentially customs duties. Postponed VAT Accounting (PVA), introduced after Brexit, allows UK VAT-registered importers to account for import VAT on their VAT return rather than paying it at the border. This significantly improves cash flow for importing retailers.

✓Postponed VAT Accounting (PVA) reconciliation
✓Monthly PVA statements from HMRC customs portal
✓Import VAT recovery on VAT returns
✓Customs duty accounting and cost of goods calculations
✓Incoterms advice for supplier contracts (DDP vs DAP)
✓CDS (Customs Declaration Service) record-keeping support

Stock & Inventory Accounting

Accurate stock accounting is essential for ecommerce businesses. The cost of goods sold (COGS) directly affects your taxable profit, and incorrect stock valuation can lead to significant tax under or overpayments. HMRC's guidance on stock valuation requires stock to be valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value.

Common Questions

Ecommerce Accountancy — Frequently Asked Questions

You must register for VAT when your taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in any rolling 12-month period. HMRC's VAT registration guidance explains the rules. For ecommerce businesses, "taxable turnover" includes all standard and zero-rated sales but excludes exempt sales. You can also register voluntarily below the threshold, which allows you to reclaim VAT on business purchases.
It depends on what you are selling and to whom. For goods sold to EU consumers, you may need to register for VAT in EU member states or use the EU's One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme if your EU sales exceed €10,000 per year. For digital services to EU consumers, OSS registration is typically required once the threshold is met. We assess your specific situation and advise on the most practical approach.
Postponed VAT Accounting (PVA) allows UK VAT-registered importers to account for import VAT on their VAT return rather than paying it at the point of importation. This significantly improves cash flow for businesses importing regularly. PVA is available to all VAT-registered businesses importing into the UK and is usually the most beneficial approach for ecommerce businesses buying stock from overseas.
Amazon FBA involves Amazon storing your stock in their fulfilment centres. If Amazon moves your stock to fulfilment centres in EU countries, this can create VAT registration obligations in those countries — even if your UK sales do not reach the OSS threshold. This is a common compliance issue for FBA sellers. We review your FBA setup and advise on any EU VAT obligations that arise from where your stock is held.
HMRC requires stock to be valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value. "Cost" includes all costs of bringing the stock to its present location and condition — typically purchase price plus import duties, freight and any other directly attributable costs (landed cost). Net realisable value is the estimated selling price less costs to complete and sell. Obsolete or slow-moving stock may need to be written down.
You do not need separate accounts for each channel, but your bookkeeping should be able to identify revenue, fees, returns and costs for each channel separately. This is important both for VAT purposes (each channel may have different VAT implications) and for understanding which channels are most profitable. We set up accounting software integrations that pull data from each platform automatically.
HMRC requires self-employed individuals and companies to keep records of all business income and expenses. For ecommerce businesses this includes sales records (settlement statements), purchase invoices, import documents, stock records and bank statements. Under Making Tax Digital, VAT records must be kept digitally. Records must generally be kept for at least 6 years.
