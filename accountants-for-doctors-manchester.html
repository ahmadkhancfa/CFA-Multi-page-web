Many salaried GPs who trained as hospital registrars have never submitted a tax return or claimed professional fee rebates going back up to four years. We review your full position and reclaim what you are owed.

Sole Trader or Limited Company?

This is one of the most important decisions a locum makes. A limited company is not always the right answer — NHS contracts, pension contributions and IR35 considerations all affect the outcome.

We assess your specific circumstances and advise on the most tax-efficient structure for your income level, working pattern and future plans — without generalising.

NI Deferral — Often Missed

Consultants with both NHS and private practice income are often paying more National Insurance than required. There is a maximum annual NI liability and many consultants exceed it — paying twice without realising.

We submit deferral applications on your behalf, recover overpayments and ensure the correct contributions are made going forward.

This is frequently missed by non-specialist accountants — resulting in unnecessary NI payments for years.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to submit a tax return as a salaried GP? +
Yes, if you have savings income, locum income, rental income, or are claiming significant professional expenses, you are legally required to submit a self-assessment return. Even without these, submitting a return is often beneficial to reclaim tax overpayments.
Should I set up a limited company as a locum? +
Not necessarily. A limited company is not always the most tax-efficient option for locums. NHS pension contributions, IR35 implications and the level of income all affect whether incorporation is beneficial. We assess your specific circumstances before making any recommendation.
Can consultants defer National Insurance on private income? +
Yes. Consultants who have already paid the maximum NI on their NHS income can apply to defer or cancel NI contributions on private practice income. We submit deferral applications and reclaim overpayments where applicable.
What expenses can doctors claim? +
Professional registration fees (GMC), Royal College subscriptions, BMA membership, medical indemnity, course and conference fees, medical equipment used exclusively for work, and business mileage are all commonly claimable. We provide all clients with a comprehensive checklist to ensure nothing is missed.
How far back can I claim professional expense rebates? +
Claims can be made up to four tax years in arrears. For doctors who trained as hospital registrars without claiming expenses, this can amount to a significant rebate. We assess your position and handle the full claims process with HMRC.
