Section 24 — Mortgage Interest Restriction

Since April 2020, individual landlords can no longer deduct mortgage interest as a business expense from their rental income. Instead, they receive a basic rate (20%) tax credit on finance costs. This is known as Section 24 of the Finance (No.2) Act 2015. HMRC's guidance explains how the restriction works.

The impact of Section 24 depends on your tax rate. Higher-rate taxpayers are most significantly affected because:

✓Rental profits are now higher (mortgage interest not deducted)
✓This pushes more income into higher-rate tax bands
✓The 20% credit only partially offsets the additional tax
✓Some landlords pay tax on a "profit" when they are actually making a cash loss after mortgage payments

Planning options include incorporating the property portfolio into a limited company (where finance costs remain fully deductible), transferring properties to a spouse in a lower tax band, or restructuring the portfolio to reduce mortgage exposure. Each option has significant tax implications that need careful modelling.

Capital Gains Tax on Property

When you sell a UK residential property that is not your main home, Capital Gains Tax applies to the gain. For 2026/27, HMRC's CGT rates for residential property are 18% (basic rate) and 24% (higher rate). The annual CGT exempt amount for 2026/27 is £3,000.

60-day reporting rule: Since October 2021, HMRC requires UK residents to report and pay CGT on UK residential property disposals within 60 days of completion. Missing this deadline results in an automatic penalty and interest charges. CFA can prepare the CGT computation and file the return within the required timeframe.

Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT)

SDLT on residential property is charged at rates from 0% to 12% depending on the purchase price. Since April 2016, purchases of additional residential properties attract a 3% SDLT surcharge on top of standard rates. For 2026/27, this remains in place and applies to buy-to-let and second home purchases.

Furnished Holiday Lets (FHL)

Furnished holiday lets that meet HMRC's qualifying conditions have historically received more favourable tax treatment than standard buy-to-let properties. The property must be available to let for at least 210 days per year, actually let for at least 105 days per year, and let commercially.

Important: The FHL regime was abolished with effect from 6 April 2025. Properties previously qualifying as FHLs are now treated as standard property lettings for tax purposes from that date. If you previously held FHLs, we can review the impact on your tax position and identify any transition planning opportunities.

Property Portfolio Incorporation

Transferring a property portfolio into a limited company can allow full mortgage interest deductibility, access to lower corporation tax rates on retained income and more flexible profit extraction. However, the transfer itself can trigger CGT and SDLT, and HMRC has specific rules on partnership-to-company incorporations using Partnership Incorporation Relief.

Annual Tax on Enveloped Dwellings (ATED)

ATED applies to UK residential properties worth more than £500,000 that are held by companies, partnerships with corporate members or collective investment vehicles. The annual charge depends on the property value and must be reported and paid by 30 April each year. Reliefs are available for property rental businesses, property developers and others.
