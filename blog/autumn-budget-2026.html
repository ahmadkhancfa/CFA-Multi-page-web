Autumn Budget 2026: What It Means for Business Owners, Landlords and Individuals

← Back to Insights
Budget & Tax 9 min read Published 29 October 2026 CFA Accountants

Chancellor John Healey delivered the Autumn Budget on 28 October 2026[1][2]. It brings changes to capital allowances, Business Asset Disposal Relief, the Trading Allowance and the tax on property and savings income. It also introduces new incentives for founders and employee share schemes.

Most of the measures start from April 2027, so there is time to plan. Below is our summary of what has changed and what it could mean for you.

1. Corporation Tax and capital allowances

The main rate of Corporation Tax stays at 25%, and full expensing for qualifying companies continues unchanged[3]. There are two changes to capital allowances[4][5]:

  • First-year allowance up to 50%. For qualifying main-rate expenditure incurred on or after 1 January 2027, the first-year allowance rises from 40% to 50%. This is most useful for unincorporated businesses and leasing businesses that cannot use full expensing.
  • Writing-down allowance down to 12%. The main-rate writing-down allowance falls from 14% to 12%, from 1 April 2027 for Corporation Tax and 6 April 2027 for Income Tax. It was already cut from 18% to 14% from April 2026.
What this means for you: If you are planning a significant purchase, the timing matters. Check which allowance applies to the asset before you commit, and make full use of the Annual Investment Allowance where you can. Company directors can also compare ways of taking profit out of the business with our salary vs dividend calculator.

2. Business Asset Disposal Relief: lifetime limit halved to £500,000

Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR) reduces the Capital Gains Tax on qualifying disposals of a business or shares in a trading company. For disposals made on or after 6 April 2027, the lifetime limit falls from £1 million to £500,000. The BADR rate stays at 18%[6][3].

At the new limit, the most anyone can save through BADR over their lifetime is £30,000 compared with the standard 24% CGT rate.

What this means for you: If you plan to sell your company, retire or restructure ownership in the next few years, review your expected gain and any BADR you have already claimed. Do not bring a sale forward for tax reasons alone, because the commercial terms and the detailed rules both matter.

3. New Founders’ Relief for high-growth companies

A new Founders’ Relief is proposed for qualifying share disposals from 6 April 2027. It would give a 10% Capital Gains Tax rate on up to £10 million of lifetime gains[3][7].

The relief is aimed at founders of high-growth, innovative companies, not owner-managed businesses in general. Under the current proposals, the individual must:

  • have held at least 25% of the company’s ordinary shares when it started trading
  • have held the shares for at least five years
  • have been UK resident for at least five tax years before the sale

The company must also meet trading and UK presence conditions and pass growth tests. The rules are out for technical consultation and may change.

What this means for you: Founders of scaling businesses who expect to raise investment or exit should follow the consultation closely. Most smaller owner-managed businesses are unlikely to qualify.

4. Trading Allowance triples to £3,000

From 6 April 2027, the Trading Allowance rises from £1,000 to £3,000. The reporting threshold for trading income under Self Assessment is also due to rise to £3,000. The Property Allowance stays at £1,000[8].

The allowance is an alternative to claiming actual expenses, so it will not suit everyone. If your costs are higher than £3,000, claiming expenses will usually be better.

What this means for you: Side businesses and occasional freelancers may no longer need to report small amounts of trading income. Keep records anyway, so you know when you cross the threshold and need to register.

5. Higher Income Tax on property and savings income

From 6 April 2027, Income Tax on property income and savings income rises by a further two percentage points, to[3]:

  • Basic rate: 24%
  • Higher rate: 44%
  • Additional rate: 49%

These rates apply only to property and savings income. Rates on employment and trading income are unchanged.

The Personal Savings Allowance still applies: £1,000 for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 for higher-rate taxpayers and nil for additional-rate taxpayers.

What landlords should do: Rework your projected rental profit after expenses and finance costs, and look at the after-tax return on each property.
What savers should do: Review how much interest falls outside ISAs and other tax-free wrappers once your allowance is used.

6. Cash ISA allowance stays at £20,000

The planned cut to the Cash ISA allowance has been reversed, and the annual ISA limit remains £20,000 from 6 April 2027[9]. The Government also plans a new ISA incentive for people investing in a Stocks and Shares ISA for the first time. Details are still to come.

7. Employee share schemes: CSOP renamed EMI2

The Company Share Option Plan (CSOP) is being renamed Enterprise Management Incentives 2 (EMI2). It keeps the CSOP name in legislation, so this is the same scheme with improved terms, not a new scheme replacing it[4][5]. From 6 April 2027:

  • The individual option limit doubles from £60,000 to £120,000.
  • The interaction with the EMI limit is removed, so employees can hold options under both schemes up to their separate limits.
  • The exercise period extends from 10 to 15 years.
  • The window to exercise EMI options after a disqualifying event extends from 90 to 180 days.
What this means for you: Growing companies get more room to reward and keep key staff. The tax benefits depend on meeting the scheme conditions and running the scheme correctly, so take advice before setting one up or changing an existing plan.

8. National Minimum Wage and National Living Wage up 3.9%

The National Minimum Wage and National Living Wage will both rise by 3.9% from April 2027[10][11].

Employers should check the rate for every employee. That includes checking how overtime, deductions and salary sacrifice affect minimum-wage compliance.

What this means for you: Build the higher pay, and the employer National Insurance that comes with it, into your overall payroll budget. Make sure your payroll software is updated before April.

9. Mileage rate rises above 10,000 miles

From 6 April 2027, the approved mileage rate for business journeys above 10,000 miles in your own car or van rises from 25p to 35p per mile. The 55p rate for the first 10,000 miles is unchanged[3].

Keep a proper mileage log and separate business journeys from ordinary commuting. For the current rules, see our guide to the HMRC mileage rate increase.

10. Business rates support for retail, hospitality and leisure (England)

From April 2027, for qualifying retail, hospitality and leisure properties only[12][13]:

  • 100% Small Business Rates Relief applies up to a rateable value of £18,000 (previously £12,000).
  • Tapered relief applies up to £21,000 (previously £15,000).

These higher thresholds run until 31 March 2029.

Eligible pubs, social clubs and most live music venues will see their relief rise from 15% to 35% for 2027/28 and 2028/29[14]. Bills for eligible retail, hospitality and leisure properties will also be held in real terms for those two years.

11. Self Assessment payments on account to change from April 2029

From April 2029, people who make payments on account will make smaller, more regular payments during the tax year, collected automatically. Eligible taxpayers will be able to choose to pay through PAYE instead[8].

What this means for you: For sole traders and landlords this is a cash-flow change. Get into the habit now of setting aside tax as you earn, rather than treating all income as yours to spend. If you are also preparing for digital reporting, read our guide to Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.

12. Household support and the State Pension

Energy. The 0% VAT rate on household electricity has been extended to 1 July 2027. Households in Council Tax bands A to D in England will receive a one-off £100 payment from January 2027. Gas standing charges are to be removed by the end of 2027 or as soon as possible after that[9][11]. Those costs move into unit rates, though, so not every household will save the full standing-charge amount.

State Pension. The full new State Pension rises by 3.9% in April 2027 to £13,036.40 a year, which is above the £12,570 Personal Allowance.

A temporary Pension Tax Allowance will apply from 6 April 2027 to 5 April 2030[15][16]. Under it, pensioners whose total income is no more than the full new State Pension pay no Income Tax. The allowance is withdrawn by £1 for every £3 of income above that level.

From April 2030, the triple lock will be adjusted. The State Pension will rise by the highest of inflation, 2.5% or the amount needed to keep its value relative to earnings[2].

What should you do next?

You do not need to change everything now, but it is worth reviewing your position before April 2027:

  1. Review your tax position for Corporation Tax, personal tax, rental income and savings.
  2. Time major purchases with the new capital allowance rules in mind.
  3. Revisit your exit plans if you expect to sell your business, and watch the Founders’ Relief consultation.
  4. Update your cash-flow forecast for higher wages and changes to tax payment dates.
  5. Check your records and payroll are ready for the new rates.

Several measures still need legislation or further guidance, so some details may change.
