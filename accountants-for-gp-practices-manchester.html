Accountancy Designed Around GP Practices

Running a GP practice involves more than preparing annual accounts. Partnership profits, partner drawings, NHS income, pensionable profits, superannuation contributions, tax liabilities and practice cash flow all need to work together.

GP practices have financial requirements that are different from many other businesses. A GP partnership may need to manage NHS income, private income, practice expenses, partner profit allocations, drawings, pensionable profits, superannuation contributions and individual partner tax liabilities at the same time.

At CFA Accountants, we provide specialist accounting and tax support designed around these requirements — working with established GP practices, new GP partnerships, incoming and outgoing partners, and practices undergoing structural changes.

Partnership Tax Returns & Profit Allocations

A GP partnership is treated differently from a limited company for UK tax purposes. The partnership return reports the partnership's results and each partner is responsible for tax on their share of the partnership profits. HMRC confirms that individual partners are responsible for the tax due on their own share of partnership profits.

We can prepare:

✓Partnership tax returns (SA800)
✓Partnership profit allocation schedules
✓Partner tax computations
✓Individual Self Assessment returns

GP Partner Self Assessment

GP partners are taxed personally on their allocated share of partnership profits rather than simply on the amount they draw from the practice. We prepare and submit GP partner Self Assessment tax returns and calculate:

✓Income Tax
✓Class 4 National Insurance
✓Payments on account
✓Balancing payments
✓Pension-related tax figures
✓Other income and deductions

For 2026/27, HMRC's guidance states that self-employed individuals with profits above £12,570 pay Class 4 National Insurance at 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% on profits above £50,270. Individual circumstances affect the final calculation.

Partner Drawings & Tax Planning

Partner drawings are not the same as taxable profit. A partner can draw money from the practice during the year, while their final taxable position is determined by their share of the partnership's taxable profit and their individual circumstances.

We prepare drawings schedules that take into account:

✓Expected partnership profit
✓Partner profit allocation
✓Drawings already taken
✓Expected Income Tax and NI
✓Pension-related amounts
✓Partner current account position
✓Expected tax payment dates
✓Practice cash flow

This helps partners make informed decisions about drawings based on the practice's financial information rather than simply looking at the bank balance.

NHS Pension, Superannuation & PCSE Support

NHS pension administration is an important part of GP partnership accounting. CFA can support GP practices and GP partners with the accounting, calculation, certification, reconciliation and administrative aspects of NHS pension and superannuation requirements.

PCSE confirms that GP and non-GP partners need to complete a Type 1 Annual Certificate of Pensionable Profits for each pension year, and that the certificate is used to ensure NHS pension contributions have been correctly paid and allocated to the member's pension record.

PCSE also provides a specific GP Pension Accountant role allowing accountants acting for GP Pension Scheme members to complete Type 1 and Type 2 forms on their behalf and check Employee Contribution Statements. The member remains responsible for logging in to complete the declaration and submit the form. Learn more at PCSE.

Our support includes:

✓Pensionable profit calculations

✓Type 2 Self Assessment support
✓Pension contribution calculations

Practice accounts → Pensionable profit calculation → Pension contribution calculation → Certificate preparation → Reconciliation → Supporting records. This gives the practice and partners a clear audit trail between the accounting records and pension submissions.

Annual Allowance & Pension Tax Calculations

GP pension arrangements can have tax implications that need to be considered alongside the partner's wider tax position. CFA can assist with tax calculations and accounting analysis relating to pension contributions and pension-related figures.

For 2026/27, HMRC states that the standard pension annual allowance is £60,000, subject to the applicable rules, including potential tapering for individuals with higher adjusted income. The standard individual lump sum allowance is £268,275. Note: the Lifetime Allowance was abolished from 6 April 2024 and replaced with new lump sum allowances.

We can help with:

✓Annual allowance calculations
✓Pension-related tax calculations
✓Pension figures for Self Assessment
✓Tax reserve calculations

Important distinction

Our service covers accounting, tax calculations, pension records, certificates, reconciliations and administration. We do not provide recommendations on specific pension investments or products. Where regulated financial advice is required, we can work alongside an appropriately authorised financial adviser. The FCA's guidance distinguishes pension administration from regulated financial advice.

Drawings themselves are not normally what determines a partner's taxable profit. A partner's tax liability is generally based on their share of the partnership's taxable profits, together with their other personal tax circumstances. HMRC confirms that each individual partner is responsible for tax on their share of partnership profits. Drawings are important for monitoring the partner's current account and cash position, but should not simply be treated as the partner's taxable income.
There is no universal amount that is appropriate for every GP partner. A sensible drawings schedule should consider the partner's expected profit allocation, previous drawings, tax liabilities, National Insurance, pension-related amounts and the practice's cash position. CFA can prepare drawings and tax reserve schedules based on the practice's financial information.
Payments on account are advance payments towards the next Self Assessment tax bill. HMRC normally requires two payments, due on 31 January and 31 July, with each payment usually being half of the previous year's relevant tax bill. This is why GP partners should consider tax reserves when reviewing drawings.
