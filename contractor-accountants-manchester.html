IR35 — Off-Payroll Working Rules

IR35 (the off-payroll working rules) determines whether a contractor working through a limited company or other intermediary should be taxed as an employee for a particular engagement. HMRC's IR35 guidance explains the rules in detail.

Since April 2021, medium and large private sector clients are responsible for determining IR35 status. For engagements with small clients, the contractor's own company remains responsible for the determination. Key factors HMRC considers include:

✓Substitution — can you send someone else to do the work
✓Control — does the client control how, when and where you work
✓Mutuality of obligation — is there an obligation to offer and accept work
✓Financial risk — do you bear financial risk for the engagement
✓Equipment — do you provide your own equipment
✓Part and parcel — are you integrated into the client's organisation

An outside-IR35 determination does not just depend on the contract wording — HMRC looks at the actual working practices. We can review both your contract and your working arrangements and advise on the overall picture. HMRC's CEST tool can also be used to check employment status for tax purposes.

Limited Company vs Sole Trader

Most contractors operate either as a sole trader or through a limited company. The right choice depends on your income level, IR35 position, liability requirements and future plans.

Limited Company

✓Potentially more tax-efficient at higher income
✓Separate legal entity — limited liability
✓Ability to retain profits in company
✓More administration and compliance costs
✓Better perception with some clients

Sole Trader

✓Simpler to set up and run
✓Lower compliance costs
✓All profits taxed as personal income
✓No limited liability protection
✓Less tax efficiency at higher profit levels

We model both options against your specific income level and circumstances before making any recommendation.

Dividend Planning & Tax-Efficient Extraction

For contractors operating outside IR35 through a limited company, taking income as a combination of salary and dividends is generally more tax-efficient than taking a full salary. For 2026/27, HMRC's dividend tax rates are 8.75% (basic rate), 33.75% (higher rate) and 39.35% (additional rate). The dividend allowance for 2026/27 is £500.

✓Optimal salary level to maximise NI efficiency
✓Dividend extraction timing and planning
✓Director loan account management
✓Corporation tax planning and reserves
✓Pension contributions from the company (highly tax-efficient)
✓Spouse or partner dividend planning where appropriate

Construction Industry Scheme (CIS)

The Construction Industry Scheme (CIS) requires contractors in the construction sector to deduct money from subcontractor payments and pass it to HMRC. Subcontractors can reclaim overpaid CIS deductions through Self Assessment or their company's corporation tax return.

✓CIS registration for contractors and subcontractors
✓Monthly CIS return filing
✓Verification of subcontractors with HMRC
✓Gross payment status applications
✓CIS deduction reclaim through Self Assessment
✓CIS compliance for limited company subcontractors

Allowable Expenses for Contractors

Contractors can claim a range of business expenses against their income, reducing their tax liability. HMRC's guidance on allowable expenses sets out the general principles. For limited company contractors, expenses must be wholly and exclusively for business purposes.

✓Professional subscriptions and memberships
✓Accountancy and professional fees
✓Business travel and mileage (HMRC approved rates)
✓Home office costs (where applicable)
✓IT equipment, software and subscriptions
✓Training and CPD courses directly related to current work
✓Business insurance (PI, PL, income protection)
✓Marketing and website costs
