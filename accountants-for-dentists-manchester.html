NHS Income, UDAs & Contract Management

NHS dental contracts are administered by NHS Business Services Authority (NHSBSA), which processes NHS dental payments and maintains contractor records. Understanding how NHS contract income is calculated, reconciled and reported is essential for accurate practice accounts.

We help dental practices with:

NHS contract income reconciliation
UDA activity analysis and reporting
Capitation and item-of-service fee reconciliation
Reconciling NHSBSA payments against practice ledgers
Clawback provisions and contract performance reviews
Mixed NHS and private income accounting

NHSBSA provides specific guidance for dental contractors on payment schedules, contract management and reporting obligations. Accurate reconciliation of NHS payments is essential for both practice accounts and correct tax reporting.

VAT on Dental Services

VAT treatment in dental practices is complex because different services attract different VAT treatment. HMRC's VAT Notice 701/57 sets out the VAT treatment of health services and specifically covers dental services.

Key VAT considerations for dental practices:

NHS dental treatment is generally exempt from VAT
Purely cosmetic dental treatment is generally standard-rated
Mixed-income practices may need partial exemption calculations
Laboratory fees and dental materials VAT recovery
VAT registration threshold monitoring for growing private practices
Correct treatment of dental plan income

HMRC's VAT Notice 701/57 provides specific guidance on which dental services qualify for VAT exemption as medical care and which are subject to standard-rate VAT as cosmetic procedures. Getting this wrong can result in either underpaid VAT or missed recovery opportunities.

Dental Associates — Tax & Self-Employment

Most dental associates operate as self-employed individuals. HMRC's Check Employment Status for Tax (CEST) tool can be used to assess whether a dental associate's working arrangements support self-employment status — though the underlying contract and working practices are what matters.

We support dental associates with:

Self-assessment tax returns
Allowable expense claims (GDC fees, indemnity, equipment, CPD)
Associate agreement review from a tax perspective
Self-employment status considerations
National Insurance planning
Pension contributions and tax relief

Buying or Selling a Dental Practice

Practice acquisitions and disposals have significant tax implications. HMRC's guidance on CGT for business assets and Business Asset Disposal Relief (formerly Entrepreneurs' Relief) can be relevant when selling a dental practice. Goodwill is typically the largest component of a dental practice sale price and is subject to specific tax treatment depending on the structure of the transaction.

Common Questions

Dental Accountancy — Frequently Asked Questions

Dental associates can claim allowable business expenses against their self-employment income. These typically include GDC registration fees, indemnity insurance premiums, CPD course fees, dental instruments and equipment used for work, and the business proportion of relevant travel costs. HMRC's guidance on expenses for self-employed individuals sets out the general principles, and we can advise on what applies to your specific circumstances.
NHS dental treatment provided under an NHS contract is generally exempt from VAT as a medical service. However, purely cosmetic dental treatment is generally standard-rated. HMRC VAT Notice 701/57 provides the detailed guidance on which dental services are exempt and which are taxable. Practices providing both NHS and private cosmetic services need to consider their VAT position carefully.
If your taxable turnover exceeds the VAT registration threshold (currently £90,000), you must register for VAT. For dental practices, only the taxable (non-exempt) element of your income counts towards the threshold. Many dental practices with significant NHS income may have limited taxable income and remain below the threshold, but this should be reviewed regularly — particularly as private cosmetic income grows.
When selling a dental practice, the key tax considerations include Capital Gains Tax on the disposal of business assets (including goodwill), and the availability of Business Asset Disposal Relief, which can reduce CGT to 10% on qualifying gains up to a lifetime limit. The structure of the sale (asset sale vs share sale for incorporated practices) significantly affects the tax outcome. Early planning is important.
Incorporation can be tax-efficient when profits significantly exceed personal drawings requirements, as retained profits in a company are subject to corporation tax rather than higher personal income tax rates. However, CQC registration requirements for dental bodies corporate, NHS contract implications and the tax cost of transferring goodwill into a company on incorporation all need to be considered. We model the numbers before making any recommendation.
Goodwill represents the value of the established patient list, reputation and ongoing earning capacity of the practice beyond its physical assets. It is typically the largest component of a dental practice's sale price. For tax purposes, goodwill created by an individual and transferred to their own company is subject to specific HMRC rules. HMRC's guidance on intangible fixed assets covers the corporation tax treatment of goodwill in companies.
Dental associates who are genuinely self-employed are taxed on their profits through Self Assessment. They pay Income Tax and Class 4 National Insurance on their net profits (income after allowable expenses). HMRC's Class 4 NI rates for 2026/27 are 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above £50,270.
HMRC can challenge self-employment status if the working arrangements resemble employment. The key factors include whether the associate can send a substitute, bears financial risk, and controls how they work. HMRC's CEST tool can be used to assess status, but the underlying contract and actual working practices are what determines the position.
